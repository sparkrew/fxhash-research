PhyllotacticGalaxies

Phyllotactic spirals form a distinctive class of patterns in nature, in general, the arrangement of leaves on a plant stem or the pattern of seeds on the face of a sunflower. This patterning can be generated via a mathematical algorithm. 

I am inspired by the Webb telescope and colorized images produced from the infrared data it obtains. These color images do not exist in nature rather they are man made the scientist so that we can visualize these glimpses of our cosmos. PhyllotacticGalaxies employs the phyllotaxis algorithm to create imaginary galaxies that simulate spatial phenomena. A micro to macro application. I hope you enjoy my interpretation of colliding galaxies made possible by the medium of algorithmic (generative) art.

Three layers of sets of 4 phyllotactic spirals are generated. Each "galaxy's" characterstics are modified by random variables: number of elements, size, translation, color and opacity. The interactions of shapes and colors are cosmic and profound. I see all kinds of images in each instance, occasionally I am reminded of Van Gogh's "Starry Nights."
